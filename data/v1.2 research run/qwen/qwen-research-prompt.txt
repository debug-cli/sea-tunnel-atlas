ROLE
You are a research analyst. Build a sourced inventory of the publicly documented surface structures of the Seattle-area light rail tunnels: emergency egress points, ventilation facilities, shafts, fan plants, and tunnel portals.

INPUT
The attached egress.geojson holds 13 known points. Treat it as the current baseline, not as truth. The other attached files (if present) hold Link station points and line alignments for context.

Current baseline, by tunnel:
• DSTT: Pine Street Ventilation Facility N00 (Pine St near 9th Ave), Westlake, Symphony, Pioneer Square, International District/Chinatown with south portal
• University Link: Capitol Hill, University of Washington
• Northgate Link: U District, Roosevelt, Maple Leaf Portal (1st Ave NE and NE 95th St)
• Beacon Hill Tunnel: Beacon Hill Station headhouse, main and ancillary shafts
• Downtown Bellevue Tunnel: East Main (south end), Bellevue Downtown (north end)

TASKS
1. Verify each baseline point. For each, find a primary source that confirms its existence, function, and location. Say whether the source agrees, disagrees, or adds detail.
2. Find missing structures. Search each tunnel end to end for portals, standalone vent buildings, shafts, and emergency exit structures the baseline lacks. Cover at least: the DSTT north portal and any DSTT vent structures outside stations, the Beacon Hill Tunnel east and west portals, the University Link and Northgate Link tunnel portals and any mid-tunnel shafts, the Downtown Bellevue Tunnel portals, and the 2 Line's use of the I-90 Mount Baker Tunnel and lid structures.
3. Record history. Note structures that were planned and dropped (for example the Montlake mid-tunnel shaft dropped in 2007), renamed, or rebuilt, with dates.
4. Record incidents tied to a specific structure (ventilation failures, closures, fan faults) with dates and sources.
5. Record reuse terms. For each source, state its license or terms of use if published.

SOURCE RANKING
Use sources in this order and name the tier for each claim:
Tier 1: Sound Transit and King County Metro documents (EIS and FEIS, final design drawings released to the public, board motions, project pages, news releases, GTFS), City of Seattle and City of Bellevue permits and land use records, WSDOT documents.
Tier 2: Contractor and engineering firm project sheets, conference papers, technical journals.
Tier 3: Local news with a named reporter.
Tier 4: Wikipedia, blogs, forums. Use these as leads only. Never use a Tier 4 source as the only source for a location.

LOCATION RULES
• Give coordinates no more precise than the source supports. Label precision as one of: exact address, parcel, intersection, block, station centroid, approximate.
• Never estimate a location from a map image alone. If a source gives only a description, give the description and set coordinates to null.
• Use WGS84 longitude, latitude.

OUTPUT
Return three parts.
Part A. A GeoJSON FeatureCollection of new or corrected features. Use the same property names as the attached file: id, name, type, tunnel, owner, public_location, visible_from_street, precision, confidence, source_title, source_url, verified, notes. Set verified to the date you accessed the source. Use type values from: vent_facility, station_egress, station_portal, portal, station_shaft, egress_structure. Use ids in the form <tunnel-prefix>-<short-name>.
Part B. A verification table for the 13 baseline points: id, verdict (confirmed, corrected, unconfirmed), source title, URL, tier, exact quote supporting the verdict.
Part C. A source list: title, publisher, date, URL, tier, license or terms, and the claims it supports.

QUALITY RULES
• Every claim needs a URL and a short exact quote from that page.
• Do not invent URLs. If a document is cited elsewhere but you cannot open it, list it as unread.
• Mark anything you could not confirm as UNVERIFIED. Mark your own reasoning as INFERRED.
• Flag any source that contradicts the baseline, and quote both sides.
• Say what you searched and found nothing for, by tunnel.
